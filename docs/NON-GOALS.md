# chalkwalk-jambot -- Non-Goals

> What the band refuses, why, and what it offers instead. A non-goal is a
> decision, not a gap -- so each fence names the principle that rejects it and
> the thing to reach for in its place.
>
> Cited as `jambot fence #N`, stable anchors. Antiphon's `fence #N` is a
> separate numbering.

## The three costs

Almost every refusal here traces to one of three:

- **The delay makes it unhearable.** You hear the band an interval late, so
  anything that has to be *perceived as a signal* arrives after it mattered
  (`JAMBOT §4`).
- **It takes the room's floor.** One person is playing; a band that competes,
  proposes or judges is spending their evening (`JAMBOT §1`).
- **It cannot be read.** A rule nobody can follow in the source is a rule
  nobody can fix at two in the morning when the band sounds wrong
  (`JAMBOT §12`).

---

## #1 Song form

**Prompted by:** the obvious idea -- head, solos, head -- and by every backing
track that works that way.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §4`.** The interval is the only unit that aligns. A verse
does not fit inside one and does not line up across several, so a form imposed
on top is a structure only the band can hear. Real jams do not have one either:
they develop themes, return to them, and drift.

**Instead:** developing variation (`JAMBOT §2`) and drifting prominence
(`JAMBOT §3`). The shape comes from material being reshaped, not from a table
of sections.

## #2 Trading solos

**Prompted by:** GenJam, which trades fours with a human and does it well.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §4` and `§1`.** A handoff is a signal, and a signal costs
two intervals of ambiguity about whose turn it is -- the human learns it is
their turn an interval after it became true. And they came to play, not to wait
for four choruses.

**Instead:** prominence drifts, so a voice becomes present over several
intervals and recedes over several more. Nobody has to notice a boundary. If
somebody wants the band out front, `band, take a solo` is a person handing the
floor over, which is a handover they can hear coming because they made it.

## #3 A personality

**Prompted by:** how easy and how tempting it is.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §9`.** Moods, opinions, jokes and emoji are all things
that are funny twice. A bot's expertise is what it actually does -- the drummer
talks about the groove, the bass player about the changes -- which is a
division of labour rather than a character, and it costs nothing.

**Instead:** names, because a name is an ADDRESS before it is charm, and
`BOT-CHAT.md` 5 needs one rare enough to match anywhere in a sentence.

## #4 An opinion about your playing

**Prompted by:** every practice tool that scores you.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §10`.** A band that grades you is not somebody to play
with, and a generative band's grade would be nonsense anyway.

**Instead:** the one question it may ask of your audio is whether it could be
heard -- silence, a click, clipping. That is a fault report, and it is bounded
to four lines said once each.

## #5 Proposing the room's settings

**Prompted by:** the band knowing the tempo is wrong for what it is playing.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §1`.** Four bots backing one person is that person having
four votes. A key, a chart and a tempo belong to the room.

**Instead:** the band follows, and when a vote already has a majority of the
humans behind it, the band casts what the room's own arithmetic needs to carry
it -- never more, and never first.

## #6 Learning you

**Prompted by:** the Continuator, which is a variable-order Markov model of the
player and is genuinely good.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §11` and `§12`.** A model of you is a mirror, and a
mirror is not somebody to play with. It also means weights, training and a
thing nobody can read.

**Instead:** analysis produces plain numbers that BIAS the generator's existing
decisions. The band plays its own material, coloured by yours.

## #7 Harmony that varies with the arrangement

**Prompted by:** wanting a bridge that actually goes somewhere.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §5`.** Sections with their own chords would have you
soloing over a progression you cannot hear, because what you hear is rotated an
interval against what you play.

**Instead:** the arrangement varies texture -- density, register, who is
present -- over one chart that everybody shares.

## #8 A bot that outlives the room's patience

**Prompted by:** nothing yet, and that is the point.

**Rejected by `JAMBOT §8`.** These can be pointed at a stranger's server. Any
bot, and the whole band, must be dismissible by anybody present, in words.

**Instead:** every exit route gets a test, and the cap on band size lives where
bots are created rather than where they are requested.
